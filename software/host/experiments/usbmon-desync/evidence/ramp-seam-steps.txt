decode_out_ramp.py -- ramp value each side of the framing-break seam, for the
--filter-nak no-load runs with a 24-bit S24 ramp played continuously into the
DUT (batch 20260905-ramp). 96 kHz -> 96 ramp frames per ms; ~10 frames per OUT
packet, so live capture advances the ramp by ~0 across a few-hundred-byte break.

  run          seam @ inner off   ramp before -> after        step across seam

  162331Z           16,245,862    3,987,940 ->  8,593,220     +4,605,280 frames = +48.0 s
                                                              (+ a +26,620-frame / +0.28 s
                                                               step ~1 KB later -- drain tail)
  161840Z                  263    (no pre-seam region)        ramp continuous through the
                                                              whole capture -- ring was
                                                              near-empty at session start

The two runs with a real pre-seam region both show the ramp ~48 s BEHIND across
the seam: the bytes before the seam were generated ~48 s earlier on the
absolute (free-running) ramp timeline than the bytes after it. That is a
previous session's data, read straight off the wire. The ~48 s is consistent
between the two, suggesting a characteristic amount of timeline the 16 MiB ring
holds (the previous session's SDRAM sink kept writing and wrapping it after its
client detached).
